Tactile Systems: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Margin expansion is absorbing near-term headwinds, signaling the company's ability to sustain profitability as core operations mature.

On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Nov. 2, 2026, Tactile Systems (TCMD -0.74%) raised its full-year adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) guidance to $50 million to $52 million. Expanding margins and double-digit growth in lymphedema treatments are offsetting temporary inventory delays in the airway clearance business. Three things management said stand out for long-term investors.

Tactile Systems expanded its sales force reach and increased clinic visits by 19% year over year, driving consistent volume growth across medical provider accounts. Expanding patient access and stabilizing Medicare reimbursement have created a reliable revenue engine that funds investments across the portfolio.

The business is streamlining how orders move from clinician referral to patient delivery by deploying artificial intelligence order management systems. Management also appointed Steve Kiewiet as Chief Operating Officer to unite patient services, order operations, and revenue cycle management. Together, those moves create capacity for higher referral volumes.

Durable medical equipment (DME), or medical device distributor, customers delayed orders while transitioning to the next-generation AffloVest untethered airway clearance system. But higher gross margins and controlled spending allowed management to absorb the temporary sales dip and still raise full-year profit expectations.

Lymphedema demand shows no signs of slowing down, giving executive leadership confidence in the core market opportunity. The business continues to expand through specialized sales teams and technological enhancements that convert referrals into shipments more efficiently.

Sustained operational execution across patient services and clinical education reinforces the company's market leadership. And as internal investments mature, management expects top-line growth to outpace operating expenses over time.
