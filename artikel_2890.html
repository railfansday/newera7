TJ Gray Wins Reno XBulls Division 2

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

PRCA bull rider TJ Gray added another win to his 2026 resume at the Reno XBulls Division 2 event this weekend, scoring 89.5 points aboard Legacy Pro Rodeo's Frenchy to claim the title and collect $8,883.

Gray topped a competitive field in Reno, finishing one point ahead of Billy Quillan, who scored 88.5 points to earn $6,810. Cody Russell finished third with 87.5 points and $5,034, followed by Cole Hill in fourth with an 87-point ride worth $3,257. Lane Vaughan rounded out the top five with an 86-point ride, collecting $2,073. Conner Halverson finished sixth with 85 points, earning $1,481, while Jate Frost, Brad Moreno, and Colton Young tied for seventh with 84.5-point rides and $691 each.

The victory in Reno is another highlight in a year that has seen Gray establish himself among the PRCA's top bull riders. After finishing No. 2 in the world in 2025, winning the NFR average and Top Gun Award with nearly $330,000 earned in Las Vegas, Gray carried that momentum into the 2026 season. He currently sits No. 5 in the world with $258,648, putting him in contention for his first world title next month.

While Gray has some ground to make up to take over the No. 1 spot in the world standings, he has experience on his side. Resistol Rookie Leader Noah Lee currently holds the top spot with $405,040.61, followed by Tristen Hutchings with $328,763.60, Luke Mackey with $280,145.48, and Stetson Wright with $279,442.91.

7. (tie) Jate Frost, Brad Moreno, and Colton Young, 84.5, $691 each

Gray's summer run began with an average title at the Snake River Stampede, where he earned more than $11,000. He followed that with a win at the Reno Rodeo Xtreme Bulls, collecting more than $18,000, and continued his success at several major summer rodeos, including California Rodeo Salinas, Ogden Pioneer Days, and Kitsap Stampede.

Gray also closed out the Xtreme Bulls season with a major victory at the Pendleton Xtreme Bulls Finale in September. He covered all three bulls for a 261.5-point aggregate, sharing the average title with Qynn Andersen. The victory paid $22,701 and marked a career milestone for the Oregon cowboy at an event he had long wanted to win.

Here at Rodeo Roundtable, we're wishing TJ and the rest of the NFR field the best of luck in Las Vegas!
