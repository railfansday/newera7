The US midterm elections are taking place today, November 3, with control of Congress at stake. Prediction markets now see nearly an 80% chance of a Democratic sweep, raising questions about how the outcome could affect Trump’s policies, financial markets and the ongoing Iran war.

The main question is whether Republicans will retain control of both chambers or Democrats will gain enough seats to challenge the administration’s agenda. According to Bull theory, Here are five possible outcomes and their potential impact on financial markets.

A Republican victory in both the House and Senate could give Trump greater support for his economic agenda. AI companies and other businesses could benefit from continued deregulation, while cryptocurrency legislation could face fewer political obstacles.

However, expectations of increased government spending and borrowing could push bond yields higher, potentially strengthening the US dollar.

A Democratic sweep could create major challenges for Trump’s administration.

Trump’s policy plans could stall: Democrats could block major bills, while Trump could veto legislation passed by Congress. This could result in two years of political deadlock.

Investigations into the administration: Democrats would regain congressional subpoena powers and could launch investigations into Trump’s family crypto ventures, tariff exemptions and political donors. Public hearings could also increase pressure on the administration.

Impeachment becomes possible: Democrats could impeach Trump through a House majority. However, removing him from office would require a two-thirds Senate majority, which Democrats are unlikely to have. Trump has said, “I’ll be impeached,” but Democratic leaders have not committed to starting proceedings.

Government funding faces uncertainty: Disagreements over the federal budget and debt ceiling could increase. Democrats could also use their influence over government spending and Senate confirmations to challenge Trump’s priorities. If both sides fail to agree on government funding, a shutdown could follow.

Iran war funding could face opposition: Democrats have promised to end the war. However, the new Congress will not take office until next year. Their main leverage could be restricting military funding rather than immediately changing US war policy.

Crypto legislation could face delays: Senator Elizabeth Warren could take a leading role in the Senate Banking Committee. The CLARITY Act could face tougher negotiations and potentially remain stalled for two years. Bitcoin’s commodity classification is likely to face less opposition than other cryptocurrency policy proposals.

US stocks could see a mixed reaction: JPMorgan sees a Democratic sweep as the weakest short-term outcome for US stocks, estimating a potential return range of -0.25% to +0.50%. However, historical performance tells a different story. Since 1954, there have been seven instances in which a Republican president faced a Democratic-controlled Congress. The S&P 500 gained in all seven periods over the following 12 months, recording an average return of 15.5%.

A divided Congress could limit major policy changes. Democrats could investigate the administration and block proposals in the House, while Republicans could resist Democratic legislation in the Senate.

For markets, uncertainty over government funding and the debt ceiling could cause short-term volatility. Cryptocurrency legislation could also face delays, although fewer major policy changes could offer some stability to businesses.

Trump could face resistance to his legislative priorities, particularly on spending and regulatory matters. Republicans could advance proposals in the House, but Democrats could slow their progress in the Senate through negotiations and opposition to key appointments.

Investors could expect a slower pace of policy changes, with market reactions depending on developments in Congress.

If the results do not establish a clear winner, uncertainty could continue until control of Congress is confirmed. Stocks, bonds and the US dollar could experience short-term volatility as investors wait for clarity.

A Democratic victory does not automatically mean a stock market decline, just as a Republican victory does not guarantee a rally.

Historical data shows that the S&P 500 was higher 12 months after all seven instances since 1954 when a Republican president faced a Democratic Congress, with an average gain of 15.5%.

The election outcome will shape expectations for US policies, but interest rates, economic conditions and corporate earnings will also determine how markets perform.
