Curreen Capital Q3 2026 Letter

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

In the third quarter, Curreen Capital was up 2%. We sold Green Dot, Siemens Energy, and Computacenter, and bought Truecaller and Clarivate. This moved our money out of stocks that had become expensive and had a lot of room to fall, and into cheaper stocks with better upside-to-downside ratios.

As noted in our Q2 letter, I had expected the Green Dot merger to close in Q2 2026, and when it did not, we sold our position. We also sold Siemens Energy (receiving â¬143.44 per share) and Computacenter (receiving Â£57.28 per share). Both stocks had risen above even my optimistic estimate of what the businesses are worth. Stocks can and do move above (and below) my valuations, but at high prices we are more vulnerable to any upset. Both businesses benefit from the boom in spending on AI data centers, and at these prices we were increasingly exposed to any slowdown in that torrent of cash. I chose to move our capital toward businesses with a greater margin of safety.

We returned to Truecaller, putting 2% of the fund into the business at SEK 16.75. We had sold Truecaller in the second quarter to add to TriNet, which I judged was further along in its turnaround. In Q3 2026, Truecaller had positive news. Its largest ad partner (Google) in its largest market changed its algorithm to increase what it paid Truecaller for ads. Previously, in Q3 2025, Google had dramatically reduced how much it paid Truecaller for ads in India, which had been a significant hit to Truecallerâs earnings. The Q3 2026 change was a possible sign of peace that improved the outlook for Truecaller. Given that new situation, the price was attractive, so we bought shares.

We also bought shares of Clarivate, an extremely cheap turnaround. We put 1% of the fund into Clarivate at $1.99 per share, and later added another 1% at $1.65.

Clarivate sells access to patent and scientific research databases (including Derwent and Web of Science) to customers including patent lawyers, academics, and research institutions. The company generates free cash flow, but it has a lot of debt and revenues have declined. Shareholders have been disappointed for a long time. At the end of the quarter, the stock was down 56% over the past year, and 92% over the past five.

Though these sound like negatives, an extended period of weak operating results and a declining stock price can set the stage for a powerful recovery. Pain can focus the boardâs attention and lead to new management (for example, Clarivateâs former CEO was replaced in August 2024), and even positive moves by management can be messy and take time to bear fruit. We began following our turnaround playbook with Clarivate. I initially valued the business months ago, and have been following it since. When the stock price went well below my downside valuation, we bought a small position (as mentioned above). When the stock price continued to fall, and went below my next buy price, we bought more. Our policy is to methodically add to these distressed businesses as the price falls further below my downside valuation. If the business improves, we will likely add more. If the stock price continues to fall, we will likely add more. And conversely, if the business gets meaningfully worse, then we will sell.

Advance Auto Parts is a retailer of replacement automotive parts and supplies. These include batteries, windshield wipers and fluid, air filters, motor oil, etc. The company has historically earned decent (approaching 20%) returns on tangible capital. The company is attempting a turnaround, and has fixed its balance sheet and is improving operations. Advance Auto currently trades at an attractive upside-to-downside ratio.

Fortrea helps its biotech and pharmaceutical customers run clinical trials with the goal of winning approval to sell new treatments in the U.S. and other countries. Fortrea helps design the trials, recruit investigators and participants, prepare data for review by regulators, and perform other tasks needed to win that approval. The company spun out of Labcorp in June 2023, and is attempting to turn around the business. Fortrea currently trades at an attractive upside-to-downside ratio.

Frontdoor sells home service plans to homeowners. The company signs on HVAC and other contractors and dispatches them when customers have problems with one of their major home appliances or systems (furnace, air conditioning, refrigerator, electrical system, etc.). Frontdoor spun out of ServiceMaster in October 2018. Frontdoor serves about 2% of U.S. homes, and uses its free cash flow to grow organically, pay down debt, repurchase shares, and acquire complementary businesses.

GetBusy provides online document exchange systems, primarily for accountants. GetBusy spun out of Reckon in August 2017, and has continued to grow since then. GetBusy does not earn money, investing through higher expenses to grow its existing businesses and attempting to launch new products. The underlying businesses are profitable and sustainable in the U.K. and U.S. GetBusy currently trades at an attractive upside-to-downside ratio.

TriNet provides health insurance, payroll, and retirement accounts to small and mid-sized businesses. The company has weathered a period of hard-to-predict medical costs, and is returning to its norm as a high-return-on-capital business that grows with health care costs. TriNet currently trades at an attractive upside-to-downside ratio.

VF Corp manages apparel brands, including The North Face, Timberland, and Vans. The company has fixed its balance sheet, improved capital allocation, and rebuilt its management team in an effort to return the business to growth. I believe that the company has good brands, the skills to manage them well, and a management team that can handle the challenges that the company faces. VF Corp currently trades at an attractive upside-to-downside ratio.
